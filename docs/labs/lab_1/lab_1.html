2.2 Data Quality Assessment

Your Task: Calculate margin of error percentages and create reliability categories.

Requirements: - Calculate MOE percentage: (margin of error / estimate) * 100 - Create reliability categories: - High Confidence: MOE < 5% - Moderate Confidence: MOE 5-10%
- Low Confidence: MOE > 10% - Create a flag for unreliable estimates (MOE > 10%)

Hint: Use mutate() with case_when() for the categories.

# Calculate MOE percentage and reliability categories using mutate()
my_state <- my_state %>%
  mutate(
    moe_pct = mhhincM / mhhincE * 100,
    reliability = case_when(
      moe_pct < 5  ~ "high",
      moe_pct <= 10 ~ "mod",
      moe_pct > 10 ~ "low"))

Part 5: Policy Recommendations

5.1 Analysis Integration and Professional Summary

Your Task: Write an executive summary that integrates findings from all four analyses.

Executive Summary Requirements: 1. Overall Pattern Identification: What are the systematic patterns across all your analyses?

In the first table, the top five counties have low reliabilities, with margin of error percentages ranging from 11.45% to 18.76%. While Alpine and Mono appear wealthier and Trinity appears poorer, the overlapping uncertainty ranges mean we cannot definitively rank them. Looking at the three selected counties, Mono and Tehama have an overwhelmingly White majority, with Hispanic populations as the second largest group. Yolo County also has a White majority, but the Hispanic population constitutes a larger share relative to Mono and Tehama. Yolo County has less than 3% Black residents, while Tehama and Mono have less than 1%. These numbers are based on the estimates; however, examining the margins of error reveals that many census tracts within these counties have data quality issues. Additionally, the number of census tracts varies widely across the counties: Mono has 4, Tehama has 14, and Yolo has 53, reflecting a large range of tract sizes within each county.

  1. Equity Assessment: Which communities face the greatest risk of algorithmic bias based on your findings?

The communities most affected by algorithmic bias are the Black and Hispanic populations. Black residents face high uncertainty in population counts, as all 71 census tracts across the three counties have data quality issues. The Hispanic population is similarly at risk, with 70 of the 71 tracts classified as low reliability, meaning their population counts are also uncertain. The White population is somewhat less affected, with 62 of the 71 tracts showing data quality issues; however, compared to the other racial groups, they face relatively lower risk.

  1. Root Cause Analysis: What underlying factors drive both data quality issues and bias risk?

Underlying factors that may drive both data quality issues and bias risk include the size of the census tracts, which can make it more difficult to accurately count populations. Tehama and Mono counties are considered rural, which may contribute to this inaccuracy. Many rural areas are underserved, receive less federal funding, and are more susceptible to data quality issues. Mono and Tehama counties also have higher margin of error percentages for median household income—18.76% and 6.95%, respectively. In contrast, Yolo County, which is not rural, has a margin of error of less than 3%, highlighting disparities in data accuracy depending on geographic location.

  1. Strategic Recommendations: What should the Department implement to address these systematic issues?

To address systematic data quality issues and bias risk, the Department of Human Services should prioritize improving data collection in rural counties by increasing resources, and targeting hard-to-reach tracts. Data quality monitoring should include regular audits and flagging high-margin-of-error areas to guide supplemental efforts. Algorithmic and policy decisions should incorporate measures of uncertainty to prevent disproportionate impacts on Black, Hispanic, and other marginalized populations. Integrating multiple data sources and using technology to identify gaps can further improve accuracy, while targeted support and community engagement ensure that interventions reach the populations most affected by unreliable data.
